# Uepaki — Category Acquisition Playbook

**For:** Sahil Mittal and the acquisition team · **Date:** 2026-10-08 · **Status:** internal operating playbook (recommendation). Not public copy.
**Data:** `UEPAKI_PRELAUNCH_PROFESSIONAL_DATABASE_V2.xlsx` (3,584 records). Counts below come from the operating overlay in `UEPAKI_SELLER_ACQUISITION_SEGMENTATION.xlsx`.
**Category names:** the database uses "Digital & Visual Arts" and "Lens & Motion"; the brief uses "Digital & Visual Artist" and "Lens & Motions". Which spelling the live category tree uses is unverified (EV-09).

### Rules that apply to every category

- **Proposition base (FD-06):** *Uepaki is a marketplace where customers can discover and buy products and connect with creative professionals for services, bookings and projects.* Describe it as what Uepaki is being built to be. Which modules (services, bookings, designs, projects) are switched on at launch is **unverified (EV-09)** — say so honestly when a service professional asks.
- **Never:** reach, ranking, visibility, lead, order, booking or income promises; "free to join"; un-cleared founding-programme numbers; referral rewards (referral launch deferred, E4); naming anyone who has not joined and consented; competitor names (E8).
- **Registration:** ₹1 + GST on web (SUB-01) — disclose whenever registration is discussed.
- **Founding programme:** use only the FP-OUT wording the founder approves (see OS report §2). The programme carries **no organic-visibility advantage**; never imply otherwise.
- **Consent:** contactable ≠ consent. First contact is 1:1 and human-written. WhatsApp only after the professional agrees to it in a reply; bulk/template WhatsApp (AiSensy) only after a recorded opt-in.
- **Credibility through specifics:** every first message names one real, specific thing about the professional's work, taken from the database's evidence fields or their portfolio.

## 1. Fashion & Lifestyle

**What the data says.** The strongest and best-documented category: 805 of 883 records have a verified own-site business email, 537 show activity in the last three months (mostly new products published on their own stores), and many are stocked by multi-designer retailers (Aza Fashions 517 associations, Pernia's Pop-Up Shop 121, Ogaan 88; FDCI membership 114). These are commercially mature labels that already sell online.

**Best segments (contact-ready).** Occasion & bridal labels (282), contemporary/prêt and womenswear (197), jewellery (78), accessories (45), menswear/groomswear (44), home & lifestyle products (15).

**Strongest cities (contact-ready).** Delhi NCR (294), Mumbai (112), Jaipur (58), Kolkata (34), Hyderabad (27), Bengaluru (21).

**Ideal early adopters.** Tier B independent labels (474 contact-ready) with their own store, recent product launches and one or two stockists — large enough to be credible, small enough to value an additional channel. Jewellery and accessories labels are good early adopters because their products ship easily and photograph well.

**Value proposition (claims-safe).** A professional place for the label's products under its own name, alongside the people who work around fashion — makeup artists, photographers, designers — in a marketplace built around identifiable makers. An *additional* channel next to their own store and stockists, not a replacement.

**Likely objections → honest responses.**
| Objection (from DB) | Response |
|---|---|
| "We already have our own store and stockists." (≈ 700 records) | "That's exactly why we're writing — Uepaki isn't meant to replace either. It's an additional channel under your own name. We'd rather tell you plainly what it is and isn't than promise numbers." |
| "Who else is on it?" | Live count + consented names only (see chicken-and-egg protocol, OS report §10). |
| "What's the commission?" | Do not quote numbers (rates unverified; ceiling blocked). "The seller terms set this out in writing; I'll send them before you decide." |
| "Who handles shipping?" | Self-ship is disabled at launch (E12); shipping runs through Uepaki's logistics arrangement once confirmed. Do not name a partner (EV-09). |

**Acquisition channel.** 1:1 founder email to the published business address; Instagram DM as second route if no reply after the sequence. Labels read email; this is a B2B conversation.

**Campaign approach.** Lead with a specific collection or recent product drop (the `Recent activity` field often names it). Use frame M-A (craft and identity) first; test M-B. Batch by city pod: approach a bridal label, a jewellery label and a menswear label in the same city in the same week.

**Founding-professional strategy.** Fashion will fill founding seats fastest; **cap it**. Recommendation: no more than ~30% of activated founding professionals from this category, so the founding community stays multi-category. Prefer labels that are also natural hubs for other categories (bridal labels work with MUAs and photographers).

**Activation strategy.** Assisted onboarding: help select the first 5–10 products that already sell on their own store, confirm shipping and payout set-up, and get a first listing live within 7 days. Activated = approved + complete profile + ≥1 live product.

## 2. Space & Design

**What the data says.** 590 records, practice-led: architecture practices (294), interior studios (202) and product/furniture/décor makers (94). Strong evidence base (The Architects Diary 483 associations, India Design ID 85, ELLE Decor India 14). 387 have a verified own-site email; **116 more have an own-site route pending re-check** — the cheapest unlock in the database.

**Best segments.** Interior design studios (134 ready), architecture practices (140), product makers (59). Product makers are the most marketplace-ready (sellable objects); studios are project/service businesses.

**Strongest cities.** Delhi NCR (105), Mumbai (56), Bengaluru (43), Ahmedabad (29), Pune (22).

**Ideal early adopters.** Tier B interior studios and small architecture practices that publish residential work and show they seek clients; furniture/lighting/object makers that exhibit (India Design ID) and sell directly.

**Value proposition.** A professional home for the practice's services and project work under the studio's own name, alongside furniture and décor makers and architecture photographers. For makers: sell pieces and take commissions under their own name. **No enquiry or lead promises** (studios will ask about leads).

**Likely objections → responses.**
| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "Our work comes from referrals." (~325 records) | "Referrals will stay your main source — we're not trying to replace them. Uepaki is a place where a client who has been referred can see your work properly, and where makers and studios can be found together." Keep it conditional; no lead claims. |
| "Small team, no time for another profile." | Offer assisted set-up from their existing portfolio (with their permission to reuse images). |
| "Project-based work doesn't fit a marketplace." | Confirm honestly which service/project flows are switched on at launch once EV-09 lands; until then, say it is being confirmed. |

**Channel.** 1:1 email; LinkedIn for practices with a company page; Instagram for makers.

**Campaign approach.** Lead with a specific project (the TAD or IDID feature cited in the evidence). Form a **Home & Space pod** per anchor city: 2 studios + 2 makers + 1 architecture/interior photographer.

**Founding strategy.** Makers first (inventory at launch), then studios. Studios are credibility anchors for the Home & Space pod; makers are the transacting base.

**Activation strategy.** For makers: first 3–5 pieces listed. For studios: profile + one service/project listing in whatever format the launch configuration supports. Run the 116-record re-check sprint before Wave 2.

## 3. Digital & Visual Arts

**What the data says.** The smallest category (129) and the most uneven: 84 verified emails, but 35 not contactable and 34 with no dated activity in 24 months. Segments: design/branding studios (53), artists/illustrators (50), calligraphy/stationery/engraving (26). Award and listicle evidence (Kyoorius, D&AD, fueler.io).

**Best segments (ready).** Design studios (35), calligraphy & stationery (15), artists/illustrators (14).

**Strongest cities.** Mumbai (23), Delhi NCR (15), Pune (10), Bengaluru (8).

**Ideal early adopters.** Calligraphy/stationery studios (they sell commissioned products into the wedding ecosystem) and independent illustrators who already sell prints or take commissions.

**Value proposition.** Sell original work and offer commissions under their own name, next to the labels, studios and couples who commission them. Never position on price or volume.

**Likely objections → responses.**
| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "Already on portfolio/freelance platforms; fear of price undercutting." (41) | Do not compare to named platforms (E8). "We are not building a bidding marketplace" is **not** a cleared claim — instead: "You set your own prices for your listings." Confirm against seller terms before saying it. |
| IP and reuse of artwork | Answer only from approved seller terms (FD-16 / EV-10 legal promises are open). |

**Channel.** 1:1 email; Instagram DM for illustrators.

**Campaign approach.** Lead with a specific work or commission. Link calligraphers to the Wedding & Occasion pod and design studios to the Brand pod (labels need identity and packaging).

**Founding strategy.** Small but valuable for diversity: target ~8% of the founding community. Prioritise people whose work also gives other categories something to buy (stationery, illustration for labels).

**Activation strategy.** One product listing (print/stationery) or one commission/service listing; portfolio of six or more images.

## 4. Beauty & Makeup

**What the data says.** The largest category (1,169) and the most important for wedding demand, but the **hardest to contact correctly**: 960 records have only a phone/WhatsApp number listed on a wedding-vendor platform (WedMeGood 958 associations). Those numbers are customer-enquiry lines; there is no consent to use them for recruitment. Only 118 are contact-ready now (99 own-site emails, 70 with a professional Instagram account). 813 publish a business WhatsApp number — contactability, not consent.

**Best segments (ready).** Independent bridal MUAs (67), makeup studios/academies (41), mehendi artists (10).

**Strongest cities (ready).** Delhi NCR (22), Mumbai (21), Chandigarh (10), Jaipur (10), Goa (8). Beauty is also deep in tier-2 cities (Udaipur 57, Chandigarh 67, Lucknow 53, Indore 48 records), mostly platform-phone-only — a Wave 3 opportunity after enrichment.

**Ideal early adopters.** Tier B independent bridal MUAs with their own website or active professional Instagram, recent client reviews and published package pricing. Studios with academies have network value (they train other artists).

**Value proposition.** A professional profile for their bridal work under their own name, next to the designers, photographers and planners couples already look for. Be explicit that service bookings on Uepaki at launch are being confirmed (EV-09) and that bookings/milestones are a paid-plan feature under current founder decisions (23 C3), with plan prices not yet confirmed (EV-03).

**Likely objections → responses.**
| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "Bookings already come through wedding portals, Instagram and WhatsApp." (~980 records) | "That makes sense — we're not asking you to move them. Uepaki is a place where your work sits next to the designers and photographers your clients are already choosing." |
| "Can I talk to clients directly / on WhatsApp?" | Policy on off-platform communication is open (SP-4a). Say: "That policy is being finalised; I'll share it in writing before you decide." |
| "It's peak season, no time." | Offer to set up in a 15-minute call and to come back after a named date. Respect it. |

**Channel.** Instagram DM from Uepaki's professional account (MUAs live there); email where an own-site address exists. **No cold WhatsApp. No calls to platform-listed numbers until legal review (Q-LEGAL-OUT).**

**Campaign approach.** Wave 0–1: only the 118 contact-ready. Before Wave 2: run the **enrichment queue** (`13_ENRICHMENT_QUEUE`, 250 highest-readiness platform-phone-only records) to find professional Instagram accounts or own-site emails. Approach **in October/early season or after peak** where possible (assumption A3 — wedding-service professionals are busiest roughly November–February; test it).

**Founding strategy.** Beauty is the strongest *network* category inside the Wedding & Occasion pod (MUAs work with designers, photographers, mehendi artists). Recruit 1–2 MUAs per anchor city early and, once activated, ask (with consent, no reward) which photographers and designers they work with.

**Activation strategy.** Profile + portfolio + one service listing in whatever format the launch supports. If bookings are paid-plan only, make the plan decision explicit in onboarding rather than discovering it later — a surprise at this step will cost trust.

## 5. Lens & Motion

**What the data says.** 565 records: wedding photo/film studios (423), architecture/interior photographers (90), commercial/fashion/product photographers (52). 357 verified own-site emails plus 114 professional Instagram routes. But 139 have no dated activity in 24 months — recency is the weak spot here; 156 are OS-P4.

**Best segments (ready).** Wedding photo/film (232), architecture/interior (41), commercial/fashion (22).

**Strongest cities (ready).** Mumbai (54), Delhi NCR (43), Chennai (35), Bengaluru (29), Hyderabad (19). Lens is unusually strong in Chennai and Bhubaneswar relative to other categories.

**Ideal early adopters.** Tier A/B wedding studios with own sites and recent work; architecture photographers who document studios already in the Home & Space pod; commercial photographers who shoot for labels.

**Value proposition.** Photographers work with every other category. Uepaki is a professional home for their services under their own name, next to the designers, studios and labels who commission them. Collaboration features may be mentioned **only** as "Planned: … Not available yet." and only if the founder lists them under RD-1a.

**Likely objections → responses.**
| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "Commission/referral-driven via architect networks." (121) | Position next to the studios they already shoot for; invite as part of the Home & Space pod. |
| "Already get wedding leads via portals/Instagram." (~260) | Same as Beauty: additional, not replacement; no lead claims. |
| Off-platform contact | SP-4a open — answer honestly. |

**Channel.** 1:1 email (studios), Instagram DM (individual photographers). Test email vs DM in Wave 2 (E05).

**Campaign approach.** Lead with a specific film/series/project. Photographers are **hub nodes**: invite them in the same week as the designers/MUAs/studios they are likely to work with in that city.

**Founding strategy.** One wedding photographer and one architecture/commercial photographer per anchor city early. Ask for consented peer introductions after activation.

**Activation strategy.** Service listing + portfolio. Re-confirm recency for the 139 stale records before any contact.

## 6. Content & Media

**What the data says.** 248 records, dominated by wedding & event emcees/anchors (162); also content creators (25), bloggers/writers (55) and production studios (6). 120 are platform-phone-only (WedMeGood/WeddingSutra), 95 have email or Instagram routes. Tier A is common (132) — many are emerging.

**Best segments (ready).** Emcees/hosts (37), content creators (16), bloggers/writers (13).

**Strongest cities (ready).** Mumbai (30), Delhi NCR (16); other cities thin.

**Ideal early adopters.** Emcees with their own site and recent event work (a service business with clear pricing); wedding content creators (reels/BTS) who already sell content production as a service.

**Value proposition.** Content and hosting as professional services under their own name — clients, not followers (Voice §3). For emcees: a place next to the wedding professionals they already work alongside.

**Likely objections → responses.**
| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "Bookings via wedding portals and Instagram." (~170) | Additional, not replacement. |
| "Works through agencies/creator platforms; wants evidence brands are on the platform." (30) | Do not claim brand demand. Say honestly that customer acquisition starts at launch and that you will share real numbers when they exist. These prospects belong in **later waves**. |

**Channel.** Email or Instagram DM; enrichment first for platform-phone-only emcees.

**Campaign approach.** Emcees inside the Wedding & Occasion pod (they meet every other vendor at events — high network potential in practice even though DB network scores are low). Bloggers/creators who need brand demand: hold until there is real customer activity to talk about.

**Founding strategy.** 1 emcee per anchor city in the pod; creators/bloggers after launch.

**Activation strategy.** Service listing with format and pricing indication; short showreel or event portfolio.

## 7. Cross-category: the three ecosystem pods

| Pod | Categories / segments | Why it works for acquisition | Contact-ready now |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wedding & Occasion** | Bridal/occasion labels, menswear, jewellery, MUAs, mehendi, wedding photographers, emcees, wedding content, calligraphy/stationery | The same customer (a couple and family) buys from all of them; professionals already work side by side and refer each other. | 822 |
| **Home & Space** | Architecture, interiors, product makers, architecture photographers, home-lifestyle labels, artists/illustrators | Studios specify makers; photographers document studios. | 403 |
| **Brand & Everyday Style** | Contemporary labels, accessories, commercial/fashion photographers, design studios, bloggers/writers, production | Labels need shoots, identity and content. | 313 |

Recruiting complementary professionals in the same city and pod at the same time is the core answer to "who else has joined?" — it gives each early joiner relevant peers without putting them next to direct competitors.
